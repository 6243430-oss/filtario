The Real Cost of a Slow Hire: $1,500/Week You're Probably Not Counting

5 min read · Updated June 2026

Most HR teams track cost-per-hire. Very few track the cost of time-to-hire. These are different numbers, and the second one is usually much bigger.

Here's what a slow hiring process actually costs — and why making it faster pays for itself immediately.

The Three Costs Nobody Adds Up

1. Lost Productivity ($500–$2,000/week per open role)

Every open role means work that isn't getting done, or work that's being absorbed by existing employees at the cost of their own output. For a $60,000/year role, the productivity cost of the position being unfilled is roughly $1,150/week. For a $40,000/year role in retail or logistics, it's around $770/week.

Multiply that by the number of open roles and the number of weeks they stay open. Most companies hiring at volume have 5-20 roles open at any time for 3-6 weeks each. That's $11,000–$240,000 in lost productivity per hiring cycle.

2. Recruiter Time ($300–$600/week per active role)

An internal recruiter spending 10 hours/week per active role (reviewing resumes, scheduling screens, conducting interviews) at a burdened cost of $35-60/hour is adding $350–$600/week in labor cost per role. At 10 open roles, that's $3,500–$6,000/week in recruiter labor — most of it on tasks that could be automated.

3. Lost Candidates (Unquantified but Real)

Top candidates — especially in competitive markets — are typically off the market within 10 days. A 30-day hiring process means you're not competing for the best talent. You're getting whoever is still available after 3 weeks. The cost here is harder to calculate but shows up in quality of hire and eventual performance.

The Simple ROI Calculation

Let's take a realistic example: a retail chain with 10 open roles at any time, average salary $45,000, typical time-to-hire 28 days.

  • Productivity cost: 10 roles × $865/week × 4 weeks = $34,600
  • Recruiter labor: 10 roles × $400/week × 4 weeks = $16,000
  • Total cost per hiring cycle: ~$50,600

Cut time-to-hire from 28 days to 7 days (3 weeks faster), and you save roughly $38,000 per hiring cycle.

An AI hiring platform at $149/month costs $1,788/year. The ROI on the first hiring cycle alone is over 20×.

Why This Calculation Gets Ignored

The productivity loss is invisible — it shows up as "everyone's a bit busier" rather than a line item on a budget. The recruiter time is seen as fixed overhead. And the lost candidates never appear in any report because you never knew who you didn't hire.

This is why hiring speed is treated as a nice-to-have rather than a financial imperative. But the math is clear: slow hiring is expensive, and faster hiring — even if it requires investment — almost always pays for itself quickly.

What to Do About It

The fastest wins come from automating the first two phases of hiring: resume screening and first-round interviews. These are the steps that take the most recruiter time and create the most candidate drop-off. Automating them with AI typically cuts time-to-hire by 60-75% without reducing quality — and often improving it, because more candidates are evaluated more consistently.
